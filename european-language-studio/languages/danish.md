# Danish — Teaching Norm

Load when `textLanguage` is `da`. Danish marks gender, number, and definiteness with suffixes and has no person agreement on verbs; the gap between spelling and pronunciation is large and should be flagged wherever it affects a learner's reading.

## 1. Orthography and Unicode

- `æ`, `ø`, `å` are distinct letters (the alphabet ends `… x y z æ ø å`). Never replace with `ae`, `oe`, `aa`.
- `å` historically written `aa` appears in names and older texts (`Aarhus`, `Blixen`); preserve in quoted text.
- **Stød** (glottal stop) is phonemically distinctive but **not written**; it distinguishes e.g. `hun` (she) from `hund` (dog). Do not invent a written marker; mention pronunciation only when it is the teaching point.
- The spelling-pronunciation gap is large: many letters are silent or reduced (e.g. `sagde` pronounced [saː], final `-d`, `-t`, `-g` often silent or soft). Give a pronunciation note for words the learner is likely to misread, not for everything.
- `c`, `q`, `w`, `x`, `z` occur mainly in loanwords and names; do not flag them.
- Quotation marks: `» «` or `" "`; preserve.
- Do not "correct" eye-dialect or regional spellings in dialogue; flag them instead.

## 2. Segmentation

Split at `.` `?` `!` and `…`. Do not split after:

- `f.eks.`, `dvs.`, `bl.a.`, `osv.`, `jf.`, `ca.`, `nr.`, `dr.`, `kl.`;
- ordinals: `den 3. maj`, `2. sal`;
- decimals and thousands: `3,14`, `1.000,50`; times `kl. 10.30`.

Headlines and slogans may be fragments; keep them as one unit. Danish sentences can be long with embedded clauses; prefer the larger unit when uncertain.

## 3. Nouns, gender, number, articles

- Two genders: **common** (n-words, `en`) and **neuter** (t-words, `et`). Gender is lexical; the article is the only reliable cue (`en bog` vs. `et hus`). Record the gender shown; do not guess.
- Definiteness is a **suffix**, not a separate article: common `-en` (`bogen`), neuter `-et` (`huset`).
- Indefinite plural: `-er` (most common nouns, `bøger`), `-e` (many neuters, `huse`), `-r` for nouns ending in unstressed `-e` (`kage → kager`); some neuters are zero (`et år → år`).
- Definite plural: plural form + `-ne` (`bøgerne`, `husene`, `kagerne`).
- **Double definiteness**: when a definite noun has a preceding adjective, add the free definite article before the adjective: `den store bog`, `det store hus`, `de store bøger`. The noun keeps its suffix — a key teaching point.
- Adjective agreement: indefinite singular neuter `-t` (`et stort hus`); `-e` for plural and all definite forms (`store huse`, `den store bog`); no ending for common singular indefinite (`en stor bog`).
- Compounds are written as one word and take the gender of the final element (`sommertur` = `sommer` + `tur`). Split transparent compounds in glosses.

## 4. Verbs

- No person/number agreement: `jeg er, du er, han er, vi er`.
- Infinitive marker `at` (`at læse`).
- Present tense: usually infinitive + `-r` (`læser, taler`); a few strong monosyllables have special forms (`er, går, står, ved`).
- Preterite: weak `-ede` (`arbejdede`, `legede`) or `-te` (`talte`, `købte`); strong with vowel change (`gik, skrev, tog`). Name the class when annotating.
- Preterite is the narrative past; perfect links past to present — state the contrast when the sentence hinges on it.
- Perfect: `har` + past participle (`har læst`); motion/change verbs take `er` + participle (`er kommet`, `er rejst`).
- **s-passive**: add `-s` (`bøger sælges her`, `det siges at`). Also deponent `-s` verbs (`synes`, `lykkes`, `mindes`). Name the type; do not gloss every `-s` as "is being …".
- Modals: `kan` (can/may), `må` (must OR may — read from context; `må ikke` = must not), `skal` (shall/will, plan), `vil` (will/want), `burde` (ought to).
- Modals take the bare infinitive (`jeg kan læse`, not `at læse`).
- Futurity is often just present + time adverbial (`jeg kommer i morgen`); do not force an English future label.
- Imperative = bare stem (`læs!`, `kom!`).

## 5. Word order (and pronouns where relevant)

- Main clause: finite verb second (V2). Inversion when a non-subject element opens the clause: `I går købte jeg en bog`.
- Subordinate clause: sentence adverbs (`ikke, altid, aldrig, ofte, sjældent, måske, bare, jo, vel, da`) precede the finite verb — the **BIFF rule** (the mnemonic for this set). `Jeg ved ikke, hvorfor han ikke kommer.` Contrast main-clause `Han kommer ikke.`
- Negation `ikke`: after the finite verb in main clauses, before it in subordinate clauses.
- Yes/no questions: verb first. Wh-questions: wh-word first, verb second.
- Personal pronouns: `jeg` (I), `du` (you sg.), `han` (he), `hun` (she), `den` (it, common), `det` (it, neuter), `vi` (we), `I` (you pl., always capitalized), `de` (they). Object forms: `mig, dig, ham, hende, os, jer, dem`. `De` (capitalized) = formal you.
- `den`/`det` also serve as the pre-adjectival definite article and as expletive subject (`det regner`).
- Reflexive pronoun `sig` for third persons (`han vasker sig`); `sig selv` = himself/herself/itself.
- Possessives: `min/mit/mine`, `din/dit/dine`, `sin/sit/sine`. `sin` refers back to the subject (`han tog sin bog` = his own; `hans bog` = someone else's).

## 6. Prepositions and valency

Record as valency, not idiom:

- `på` (on/at — surfaces and many places: `på skolen`); `i` (in — enclosed places, towns, countries: `i Danmark`, `i København`); `af` (of/by/from); `til` (to); `for` (for); `om` (about/around/in + time: `om sommeren`); `med` (with); `gennem` (through).
- `hos` = at someone's place / with a person (`hos os` = at our place); do not confuse with `på`/`i`.
- Time: `i` + year/month/season (`i 2025`, `i januar`), `på` + day (`på mandag`), `om` + general period (`om morgenen`).
- Verb government: `vente på`, `tænke på`, `lede efter`, `se på`, `høre på`, `tale om`, `spørge om`, `komme fra`, `bo i/på`, `glæde sig til`.
- Adjective government: `bange for`, `glad for` (happy about / fond of), `stolt af`, `interesseret i`, `afhængig af`.

## 7. Register and colloquial forms

- `du` vs. `De`: `De` (capitalized) is formal, now mainly in official letters and older usage; default to `du`. Note `De` only when it appears.
- Informal tone is common in public writing; do not over-mark as slang.
- Spoken/colloquial reductions in dialogue: `'kke` (ikke), `sku'` (skulle), `ha'` (have), `så'n` (sådan). Mark `informal`, not error.
- Discourse markers: `jo` (as you know / after all), `vel` (surely), `da` (then — many functions), `nok` (probably), `altså` (so / that is). Gloss functionally; do not translate `jo` as English "yes" out of context.
- Swear and intensity words (`forbandet`, `pokkers`, `sindsygt`) — mark sensitivity; do not recommend productive use.

## 8. Regional variation

- Standard written Danish (rigsmål) is the default; regional variation is minor compared to Norwegian, mostly in pronunciation and a modest lexicon set.
- Jutlandic, Bornholm, and Sønderjysk features appear mostly in speech and stylized dialogue; label only with evidence.
- These examples are illustrations, not a region assignment; apply them only when the source supports them.
- Label region only with evidence from the source or a cited dictionary; otherwise `regionCertainty: "unverified"`. Do not infer region from spelling alone.

## 9. Common learner errors and false friends

- `rolig` = calm. Not Swedish `rolig` = funny; "funny" is `sjov`.
- `rar` = nice/kind/pleasant. Not English "rare" (= `sjælden`); not Norwegian `rar` = weird/odd.
- `artig` = polite/well-behaved. Not English "arty"; not Norwegian `artig` = fun/amusing.
- `blød` = soft; `by` = town/city (English "by" = `ved`/`hos`).
- `glas` = glass (material/drinking vessel: `et glas`); ice cream is `is`/`flødeis`. Swedish `glass` = ice cream — do not transfer.
- `fart` = speed; English "fart" = `prut`/`fjert`.
- `fuld` = drunk; "full" (of food) = `mæt`.
- `men` = but; English "men" = `mænd`.
- `bare` = only/just; English "bare" = `nøgen`.
- `gift` = married (adjective) or poison (noun) — read from context.
- `middag` = dinner (main meal), not "midday".
- `aktuelt` = current/relevant, not "actual" (`faktisk`).
- `eventuelt` = possibly, not "eventually" (`til sidst`).
- Learner errors: dropping V2 inversion; placing `ikke` after the verb in subordinate clauses; `sin` vs. `hans/hendes`; `en`/`et` gender errors; omitting double definiteness; wrong `har`/`er` auxiliary; pronouncing as spelled (flag the silent-letter/stød cases that block comprehension).

## 10. Teaching priorities for Danish texts

1. Definiteness: suffix article and double definiteness.
2. V2 inversion and subordinate-clause adverb position (BIFF).
3. Common/neuter gender and adjective agreement.
4. `har` vs. `er` perfect auxiliary and the s-passive.
5. Preposition choice (`i`/`på`, verb government).
6. Spelling-pronunciation mismatches that affect reading, plus false friends (rar, artig, glas, fart).

## 11. Annotation checklist

- [ ] Gender (common/neuter), number, and definiteness noted for annotated nouns; double definiteness explained when an adjective precedes.
- [ ] Verb tense identified; strong/weak preterite class and auxiliary (`har`/`er`) stated.
- [ ] s-passive type named, not glossed as generic passive.
- [ ] Word order explained: V2 or subordinate-clause adverb position.
- [ ] `æ`/`ø`/`å` preserved; silent-letter/stød pronunciation noted only where it affects reading.
- [ ] `sin` vs. `hans/hendes` clarified if it appears.
- [ ] Regional label only with evidence; otherwise unverified.
- [ ] No CEFR or frequency claim.
