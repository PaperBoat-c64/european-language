# Portuguese — Teaching Norm

Load when `textLanguage` is `pt`. Portuguese marks gender, number, person, tense, and mood; the European and Brazilian standards differ substantially in grammar, lexis, and clitic placement. Do not smooth those differences away.

## 1. Orthography and Unicode

- Diacritics: acute `á é í ó ú` (open vowel + stress), circumflex `â ê ô` (closed vowel), grave `à` (only in crase, `a` + `a`), tilde `ã õ` (nasal vowels: `não`, `mão`, `irmã`), cedilla `ç` (only before `a/o/u`: `coração`, `lição`).
- No `ñ`. Palatal /ɲ/ is `nh` (`manhã`, `senhora`); /ʎ/ is `lh` (`filho`, `trabalho`). `s` between vowels is /z/ (`casa`); /s/ between vowels is `ss` (`passar`).
- 1990 Acordo Ortográfico: the trema was dropped (`frequente`, not `freqüente`) and many silent consonants removed (`ótimo`, not `óptimo`; `ato`, not `acto`; `ação`, not `acção`). Preserve the source's spelling; if pre-Acordo, keep it and note it rather than normalizing.
- Hyphenation in clitics is grammatical: `amo-o`, `dar-te-ei`, `levá-lo`, `fazê-lo`, `têm-no`. After `-r/-s/-z`, `o/a` becomes `-lo/-la`; after nasal endings, `o/a` becomes `-no/-na`.
- Nasal diphthongs: `ão` (`pão`), `õe` (`põe`), `ãe` (`mãe`), and the plural `ões`. Do not confuse `ão`/`õe`/`ãe`.
- `gu`/`qu` before `e`/`i` often have a silent `u` (`guerra`, `que`, `quilo`); the trema once marked a pronounced `u` (`qüinqüênio`) but was dropped by the Acordo.
- Quotation marks: `“ ”` (EP) and `" "` (BR) both occur. Preserve.

## 2. Segmentation

Split at `.` `?` `!` and `…`/`...`. Do not split after:

- `Sr.`, `Sra.`, `Srta.`, `Dr.`, `Dra.`, `Prof.`, `Eng.`, `Av.`, `etc.`, `p. ex.`, `n.º`, `séc.`;
- decimals and thousands: `3,14`, `1.000,50` (BR) or `1 000,50` (EP) — preserve the source's spacing;
- initials.

Portuguese sentences can be long; prefer the larger unit.

## 3. Nouns, gender, number, articles

- Gender is lexical. `-o` usually masculine, `-a` feminine, `-e` either (`o dente`, `a noite`). `-gem` is feminine (`a viagem`, `a imagem`); `-ção`/`-são` feminine (`a lição`, `a canção`); `-dade`/`-idade` feminine (`a cidade`, `a verdade`); `-ão` is often masculine but many are feminine (`a mão`, `a razão`).
- Plurals: `-s` after a vowel (`casa → casas`), `-es` after consonant/`r`/`z` (`flor → flores`, `rapaz → rapazes`, `mar → mares`); final `-l → -is` (`animal → animais`, `papel → papéis`, `azul → azuis`); final `-m → -ns` (`homem → homens`, `jovem → jovens`).
- `-ão` plurals are irregular and must be learned: `-ões` (most: `coração → corações`), `-ães` (`pão → pães`, `cão → cães`), `-ãos` (`mão → mãos`, `irmão → irmãos`, `órgão → órgãos`). Record the actual plural; do not guess.
- Invariable: `o lápis → os lápis`, `o ônibus → os ônibus`.
- Derivational size/affection: diminutive `-inho/-inha` (`casa → casinha`) and augmentative `-ão/-ona` (`livro → livrão`); note these as word-formation, not inflection.
- Definite articles: `o/a/os/as`; indefinite `um/uma/uns/umas` (`um`/`uma` also mean "one"/"a certain"; `uns`/`umas` = "some"/"a few").
- Contractions: `a + o = ao`, `a + a = à` (crase), `a + os/as = aos/às`; `de + o/a = do/da`, `de + os/as = dos/das`; `em + o/a = no/na`, `em + os/as = nos/nas`; `por + o/a = pelo/pela`, `por + os/as = pelos/pelas`. Expand them in glosses (`no` → `em + o`). `com` + article is not normally contracted in writing.
- Crase (`à`) marks the fusion `a` (to) + `a` (the): `vou à praia`. Only when both `a`s are present; do not add it elsewhere.

## 4. Verbs

- Three conjugations: `-ar` (`falar`), `-er` (`comer`), `-ir` (`partir`), plus `pôr` and derivatives (`supor`, `compor`). `ter` (to have) and `haver` (there to be / auxiliary in formal writing).
- `ser` vs `estar`: `ser` = identity, origin, inherent traits, time, events (`o concerto é no parque`); `estar` = location, temporary state, condition. `estar + a + infinitive` (EP) or `estar + gerúndio` (BR) = progressive (`estou a ler` / `estou lendo`). `ficar` = to become / stay / be located.
- Personal infinitive (infinitivo pessoal): the infinitive inflects for person (`para eu fazer`, `é melhor vocês irem`, `antes de sairmos`). This has no English equivalent; note it when it appears and explain the subject it agrees with.
- Futuro do subjuntivo (future subjunctive): `se eu fizer`, `quando for`, `se você quiser` — used after `se`, `quando`, `enquanto`, `assim que` for future/hypothetical conditions. Distinct from the present subjunctive; do not merge them.
- Present perfect = `ter` + past participle (`tenho feito`, `tenho trabalhado`) = ongoing/repeated action up to the present — different from the English and Spanish present perfect. Flag this explicitly.
- Preterite (pretérito perfeito: `fui`, `fez`) vs imperfect (pretérito imperfeito: `era`, `fazia`): completed event vs background/habit/description.
- Participles: regular `-ado`/`-ido`; irregular `aberto` (abrir), `escrito` (escrever), `feito` (fazer), `visto` (ver), `posto` (pôr), `dito` (dizer), `pago` (pagar). Some verbs have double participles (`eleito`/`elegido`); BR often uses `aceito` where EP uses `aceite`.
- `ter` vs `haver`: EP uses `ter` for possession and `haver` for existence (`há`); BR uses `ter` for both (`tem` = there is). Note the variety.
- Future and conditional: synthetic forms exist (`farei`, `faria`) but BR speech prefers `vou falar`/`iria falar`; EP uses mesoclisis in these tenses (`dar-te-ei`). Negative imperatives use the subjunctive.
- Subjunctive triggers: `espero que`, `quero que`, `duvido que`, `talvez`, `embora`, `para que`, `caso`, `antes que`. Identify the trigger.
- `ir` + infinitive = near future (`vou falar`); `poder`/`dever` + infinitive for ability/obligation; `haver de` + infinitive for a formal/promise-like future. The pretérito mais-que-perfeito (`falara`) is literary/formal.
- Passive: `ser` + past participle = action (`foi escrito`); `estar` + past participle = resultant state (`está escrito`). Distinguish.

## 5. Pronouns and clitics

- Subject pronouns: `eu, tu, você, ele/ela, nós, vós, vocês, eles/elas`. `você(s)` takes third-person verb forms; `vós` is archaic/liturgical or regional (northern Portugal).
- Object pronouns: `me, te, o/a, lhe, nos, vos, os/as, lhes`; reflexive `se`. `o/a/os/as` are direct objects; `lhe/lhes` indirect. `o/a` becomes `lo/la/los/las` after `-r/-s/-z` (`fazê-lo`) and `no/na/nos/nas` after nasal endings (`têm-no`).
- Enclisis (hyphenated, default in EP): `amo-o`, `dou-lhe`, `chama-se`, `lavar-se`. Mesoclisis (EP future/conditional): `dar-te-ei`, `dir-me-ia`. Proclisis (default in BR, and in EP after negatives, subordinators, interrogatives, and `que`): `me dá`, `não o vi`, `que me dizes?`, `quando me viu`.
- Combined: `mo/ma/mos/mas` (`me` + `o/a`): `deu-mo`; `lho/lha` (`lhe` + `o/a`). Rare in speech; show the expansion.
- Distinguish the clitic `o/a` (hyphenated after the verb: `amo-o`) from the article `o/a` (before a noun: `amo o livro`). Same spelling, different words.
- Never start a standard EP sentence with an object clitic; BR freely does (`Me dá o livro`).
- Impersonal/passive `se`: `vende-se casa` / `vendem-se casas` (agreement with the plural object).
- Reflexive placement varies: EP prefers enclisis (`chama-se`), BR proclisis (`se chama`). Mark the variety.
- Prepositional stressed forms: `mim`, `ti`, `si`, `ele/ela`, `nós`, `vós`, `eles/elas` (`para mim`, `para ti`). `com` fuses: `comigo`, `contigo`, `consigo`, `conosco`, `convosco`.

## 6. Prepositions and valency

- `a` (to/at), `de` (of/from), `em` (in/on/at), `por` (for/through/by), `para` (for/to, purpose/destination), `com` (with), `sem` (without), `sobre` (on/about), `entre` (between/among).
- `por` vs `para`: `por` = cause, agent, duration, movement through; `para` = purpose, destination, recipient, deadline. Explain the relation in the sentence, not one English gloss.
- Direction uses `a` or `para` (`ir a/para Lisboa`); location uses `em` (`estar em Lisboa`, `no Brasil`). Some country names take the article (`o Brasil`, `a França`, `os Estados Unidos`); others do not (`Portugal`, `Angola`). Note the article in contractions (`no Brasil`, `na França`).
- Time: `há` (or BP `tem`) + duration = "ago" (`há dois anos` = two years ago); `daqui a` + duration = "from now" (`daqui a dois anos`).
- Fixed valency: `gostar de`, `precisar de`, `depender de`, `pensar em`, `acreditar em`, `preocupar-se com`, `namorar com` (EP) / `namorar` (BR), `ir a/para`, `chegar a`, `começar a`, `acabar de`, `consistir em`, `interessar-se por`.
- Double-object verbs: `dar algo a alguém`; the recipient is indirect (`dou-lhe o livro`).

## 7. Register and colloquial forms

- Address: `você` (BR general; EP neutral-semi-formal, can sound blunt to intimates), `tu` (EP informal with proper verb agreement; BR regional or colloquial, often with third-person verb forms), `o senhor`/`a senhora` (formal, both varieties), `vós` (archaic/liturgical/northern Portugal).
- BR informal `a gente` = "we" (third-person singular verb: `a gente vai`). Note it as informal.
- Capitalized `Você`/`O Senhor` in writing marks formal address; preserve the source's capitalization.
- Discourse markers: `então` (then/so), `pois` (well/then, EP), `né` (BR tag, from `não é`), `ora`, `bem`, `olha` — gloss functionally.
- Slang is regional and fast-moving; label region and mark `needsVerification` unless well established.
- `você`/`tu` mixing in BR is a register phenomenon, not an error; describe it, do not correct it.

## 8. Regional variation

- European (EP) vs Brazilian (BP) Portuguese differ in grammar, lexis, and spelling. Label only with evidence from the source or a cited dictionary.
- Lexical contrasts: `autocarro`/`ônibus` (bus), `comboio`/`trem` (train), `pequeno-almoço`/`café da manhã` (breakfast), `casa de banho`/`banheiro` (bathroom), `telemóvel`/`celular` (mobile phone), `gelado`/`sorvete` (ice cream), `fato`/`terno` (suit). `bicha` = queue in EP but a slur in BP — mark sensitivity.
- Grammatical contrasts: clitic placement (enclisis/mesoclisis vs proclisis), progressive (`a` + infinitive vs gerund), `ter`/`haver`, `você`/`tu`, second-person verb agreement in BP.
- African and Asian Portuguese (Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde, Timor-Leste) have their own norms; do not generalize EP rules to them.
- When the variety is not identifiable, use `regionCertainty: "unverified"`. Never call a Brazilian or African form "wrong Portuguese".

## 9. Common learner errors and false friends

- `atualmente` = currently, not "actually" (`na verdade`).
- `esquisito` = strange, not "exquisite" (`requintado`).
- `embaraçada` = embarrassed/awkward, not "pregnant" (`grávida`).
- `puxar` = to pull, not "push" (`empurrar`).
- `realizar` = to carry out/achieve; "to realize" (become aware) = `perceber`/`dar-se conta`.
- `pretender` = to intend; "to pretend" = `fingir`.
- `livraria` = bookshop; "library" = `biblioteca`.
- `parentes` = relatives; "parents" = `pais`.
- `fábrica` = factory; "fabric" = `tecido`.
- `assistir` = to watch/attend (`assistir a`); "to assist" = `ajudar`.
- `costume` = habit/custom; "costume" = `fantasia`. `novela` = soap opera/telenovela; "novel" = `romance`. `pasta` = folder/briefcase; "pasta" = `massa`. `sapato` = shoe. `idioma` = language.
- `ser`/`estar` and `por`/`para` misuse; preterite vs imperfect confusion; wrong subjunctive trigger; omitting the personal infinitive where required; `ter` vs `haver`; `-gem`/`-ção` gender errors; crase misuse; misspelling nasal diphthongs; confusing `por que/porque/porquê/por quê`, `mas/más/mais`, `dele/do`.

## 10. Teaching priorities for Portuguese texts

1. `ser`/`estar` and `por`/`para` where the sentence hinges on them.
2. Preterite vs imperfect and the personal infinitive.
3. Future subjunctive and its triggers.
4. Clitic placement: enclisis, mesoclisis, proclisis, and the variety that licenses them.
5. Present perfect (`ter` + participle) meaning vs English.
6. EP/BP marking with evidence.

## 11. Annotation checklist

- [ ] Gender and number given for annotated nouns; `-ão` plural recorded.
- [ ] Verb tense/mood/person stated; subjunctive or personal-infinitive trigger identified.
- [ ] `ser`/`estar` and `por`/`para` choice explained.
- [ ] Clitic placement named (enclisis/mesoclisis/proclisis) and the governing factor stated.
- [ ] Contraction expanded (`no` → `em + o`); crase justified.
- [ ] Diacritics, tilde, cedilla, and hyphenation preserved from source.
- [ ] EP/BP label only with evidence; otherwise unverified.
- [ ] No CEFR or frequency claim.
