# Swedish — Teaching Norm

Load when `textLanguage` is `sv`. Swedish has two genders, a suffixed definite article with double definiteness, no verb agreement, and strict V2 word order with a distinctive subordinate-clause negation rule. Do not apply German or English article logic.

## 1. Orthography and Unicode

- `å ä ö` are distinct letters at the end of the alphabet, not `a`/`o` with diacritics. Never replace with `a`/`o` or with `aa`/`ae`/`oe` in quoted text; preserve exactly.
- No other native accents. `é` occurs only in loanwords and names (`idé`, `armé`, `café`, `allé`) and in `én` ("one") for disambiguation; keep it where the source has it.
- No noun capitalization: capitalize only proper nouns, sentence starts, and the formal `Ni`.
- No possessive apostrophe: the genitive `-s` attaches directly (`Annas bok`, `Sveriges huvudstad`). Apostrophes appear only in loanwords or quoted speech.
- Quotation marks: `" "` (some publications use `„ "`); preserve.

## 2. Segmentation

Split at `.` `?` `!` and `…`. Do not split after:

- abbreviations: `t.ex.`, `dvs.`, `bl.a.`, `osv.`, `t.o.m.`, `fr.o.m.`, `m.m.`, `nr`, `ca`, `dr`, `prof`;
- ordinals: `3:e`, `1:a`, `den 3 maj`;
- numbers: `3,14`, `1 000,50`, `kl. 10.30`;
- initials.

Swedish sentences may chain clauses; prefer the larger unit when the boundary is uncertain.

## 3. Nouns, gender, number, articles

- Two genders: common (`en`) and neuter (`ett`). Gender is lexical and mostly not predictable from meaning; compounds take the gender of the last element.
- Plural endings: `-or` (`flicka → flickor`), `-ar` (`pojke → pojkar`), `-er` (`sak → saker`), `-n` (`äpple → äpplen`), zero (`barn → barn`, `lärare → lärare`). Some nouns also change the stem vowel (`man → män`, `hand → händer`, `bok → böcker`). Record the plural with the noun.
- Genitive: `-s` appended to the whole noun phrase, no apostrophe (`min brors bil`, `mannens hund`).
- Definiteness is a suffix: `en bok → boken`, `ett hus → huset`, plural `böckerna`, `husen`. There is no separate definite article word.
- Double definiteness: a definite noun modified by an adjective takes the preposed `den/det/de` PLUS the suffixed article: `den stora boken`, `det stora huset`, `de stora böckerna`.
- Indefinite: `en`/`ett`; no indefinite plural article (bare noun: `jag ser hus`).
- Adjectives agree: common `en stor bok`, neuter `ett stort hus`, plural/definite `stora böcker`, `den stora boken`.

## 4. Verbs

- No person/number agreement: `jag är, du är, han är, vi är`. One present form for all persons.
- Present tense: mostly `-r` added to the stem (`talar`, `läser`, `kommer`); strong verbs often have a short stem-only form (`går`, `ser`, `gör`, `vet`). Present also expresses future with a time expression (`jag åker i morgon`).
- Preterite (past): weak verbs in `-de` (`talade`) or `-te` after voiceless stems (`läste`, `köpte`); strong verbs change the stem vowel (`skriva → skrev`, `springa → sprang`, `gå → gick`).
- Perfect: `har + supine` (`har talat`, `har skrivit`); pluperfect: `hade + supine`. The supine is a distinct form (`skrivit`), not the past participle (`skriven/skrivet/skrivna`).
- `s`-passive: `-s` on the verb (`boken skrivs` = "the book is written", `dörren öppnas`); also deponent verbs that exist only with `-s` (`hoppas` = hope, `finns` = exists, `andas` = breathe, `minns` = remember). Do not gloss `-s` as reflexive.
- Future auxiliaries: `ska` (intention/promise), `kommer att` (prediction/neutral), or present + time expression. Modals `kan, måste, får, vill, bör` take a bare infinitive.
- Reflexive verbs use `sig` (`sätter sig`, `klär på sig`); reciprocity uses `varandra`/`varann` (`de träffas`).
- `håller på att + inf` = ongoing action.

## 5. Word order and pronouns

- Main clause V2: the finite verb is second; a non-subject element in first position triggers inversion (`Jag läser tidningen` / `I går läste jag tidningen`). Coordinate conjunctions `och, men, eller, för` do not invert.
- Subordinate clause BIFF rule ("i bisats kommer inte före finit verb"): sentence adverbials such as `inte, aldrig, alltid, ofta, redan, nog, kanske` precede the finite verb (`…att han inte kommer`, `…eftersom jag alltid läser`), whereas in main clauses they follow it (`Han kommer inte`). This is the key word-order rule for English learners.
- Subordinate clauses otherwise keep subject–verb order (no German-style verb-final).
- Yes/no questions: verb first (`Kommer du?`); wh-questions: wh-word first, verb second.
- Pronouns: `jag, du, han, hon, den, det, vi, ni, de`. Object forms: `mig, dig, honom, henne, den, det, oss, er, dem`. `de`/`dem` are both often written `dom` in informal texts — keep and note the source form.
- `det` (neuter) vs `den` (common): pronoun choice follows the noun's gender (`ett hus → det`, `en bok → den`).
- `man` = impersonal "one/people" (`man säger`), distinct from `en man` (a man).
- `sin/sitt/sina` = reflexive possessive referring to the subject (`han tvättar sin bil` = his own car) vs `hans/hennes` (someone else's). A classic learner error.
- `det` dummy subject (`det regnar`, `det är kallt`, `det finns` = there is/are).

## 6. Prepositions and valency

Record fixed patterns as valency:

- `tänka på`, `vänta på`, `leta efter`, `prata om`, `lyssna på`, `titta på`, `bero på`, `höra av sig till`;
- `på` for many locations and times (`på stan`, `på måndag`, `på svenska`); `i` for enclosed spaces, countries, and cities (`i Sverige`, `i Göteborg`);
- `av` = of/from/by (agent); `till` = to; `för` = for; `om` = about / in a period of time (`om en vecka`); `med` = with; `genom` = through; `under` = during/under; `över` = over/across;
- distinguish `tycka om` (like) from `tycka` (to opine) and `tänka` (to think).

## 7. Register and colloquial forms

- `du` is the universal singular "you"; `ni` is plural or, in older/very formal usage, a singular polite `Ni`. In Finland Swedish `ni` remains a singular polite form. Note the text's usage; do not assume `ni` = plural without context.
- Colloquial/spoken: `dom` for `de/dem`, `mej/dej` for `mig/dig` (informal spelling), `nånting`/`nåt` for `någonting`, `sån`/`såna` for `sådan`/`sådana`. Mark `informal`, not errors.
- `väl`, `ju`, `nog`, `då` are stance particles with no direct translation; gloss their function (`ju` ≈ "as you know/of course").

## 8. Regional variation

- Standard `rikssvenska` is a written/pronunciation norm; regional accents and vocabulary exist but are rarely written.
- Finland Swedish (`finlandssvenska`) differs lexically and pragmatically: `råka` (to meet/come across), `batteri` (radiator), and `ni` as a polite singular. Label only with evidence; otherwise `regionCertainty: "unverified"`.
- Some regional words are dialectal (Scanian, Norrlandic); do not assign a region from a standard form. Sami, Finnish, and Meänkieli are separate languages, not Swedish dialects.

## 9. Common learner errors and false friends

False friends (relative to English):

- `rolig` = funny/enjoyable (older Swedish also "calm"); not "rolling".
- `semester` = vacation; the academic "semester/term" is `termin`.
- `gift` = married (adjective); as a noun it also means "poison". "Present/gift" is `gåva`/`present`.
- `by` = village; the preposition "by" is `av`/`förbi`.
- `fart` = speed; "fart" is not a Swedish word.
- `bra` = good; "bra" (brassiere) is `behå`.
- `kostym` = (men's) suit; "costume" is `dräkt` (or `kostym` only in theater/film contexts).
- `glass` = ice cream; the material "glass" is `glas`.
- `full` = drunk; "full" is `fullständig`/`mätt` (sated).
- `barn` = child/children; `kind` = cheek; `ost` = cheese; `varm` = warm; `kort` = card/short; `slut` = end/finished; `öl` = beer (not "oil" = `olja`).

Learner errors:

- Adding an English-style 3rd-person marker; Swedish has no person/number agreement.
- Forgetting the suffixed definite article or writing `*den bok` without the suffix; missing double definiteness (`*den stor bok` → `den stora boken`).
- Adjective agreement `-t`/`-a`; `det` vs `den`.
- `sin` vs `hans/hennes`.
- BIFF violations: placing `inte` after the finite verb in a subordinate clause.
- V2 violations: no inversion after a fronted adverbial.
- Supine vs past participle (`har skrivit`, not `*har skriven`).
- `ska` vs `kommer att` vs present for future; `tycka om` vs `tycka` vs `tänka`.

## 10. Teaching priorities for Swedish texts

1. Definiteness: the suffixed article and double definiteness.
2. BIFF word order in subordinate clauses; V2 inversion.
3. Adjective agreement and `det`/`den` choice.
4. `sin/sitt/sina` and pronoun reference.
5. Verb forms: preterite vs supine; `s`-passive and deponents.
6. Future and modal choices (`ska`, `kommer att`, `får`, `måste`).

## 11. Annotation checklist

- [ ] Gender (`en`/`ett`) and plural class given for annotated nouns.
- [ ] Definite form shown (`boken`, `huset`); double definiteness recorded when an adjective is present.
- [ ] Verb form identified (present, preterite, supine); no agreement invented.
- [ ] `s`-passive vs deponent vs reflexive distinguished.
- [ ] BIFF and V2 explained where the order differs from English.
- [ ] `sin` vs `hans/hennes` and `det`/`den` reference resolved.
